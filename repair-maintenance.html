Common Repairs

We Fix Every Garage Door Problem

Whatever is wrong with your door — we've fixed it before. Call Edgar and describe the problem. He'll tell you exactly what's needed and how much it costs.

Off-Track Doors

Door jumped off the rails or is binding on one side? We realign the tracks, adjust rollers, and get your door running smoothly again.

Broken Cables

Frayed or snapped lift cables cause uneven operation and safety risks. We replace both cables and inspect the drum and pulleys.

Track Alignment

Bent or misaligned tracks make your door loud, slow, and unsafe. We straighten, realign, and re-fasten tracks to factory spec.

Panel Dent Repair

One or two dented or cracked panels? We can often replace individual sections without replacing the entire door — saving you money.

Noisy Doors

Squeaking, grinding, or rattling? Usually a quick lubrication, roller replacement, or hardware tightening is all it takes to restore quiet operation.

Sensor Problems

Door reverses before closing, blinks, or won't close at all? We diagnose, align, and replace photo-eye sensors — often in minutes.

Hardware & Rollers

Worn rollers, broken hinges, and loose hardware cause noise and premature wear. We replace all worn parts and re-lubricate everything.

Weatherstripping

Gaps at the bottom or sides of your door let in cold air, pests, and water. We replace weatherstripping on all four sides.

Opener Issues

Opener runs but door won't move, or works intermittently? We diagnose opener motor, gear, and wiring issues before recommending repair or replacement.

FAQ

Repair & Maintenance Questions

My door is stuck halfway open — is that an emergency? +

It depends on your security situation. If your door is stuck open overnight, call Edgar for priority service. For stuck-closed doors where you can still access via a side entry, next-day service is usually fine. Either way, don't force the door — call us first.

Should I repair or replace my old garage door? +

If your door is under 15 years old and structurally sound, repairs are almost always more cost-effective. If it's older, severely damaged, or has significant rust or rot, replacement may make more sense long-term. Edgar will give you his honest opinion either way.

How often should I have my garage door serviced? +

We recommend an annual tune-up for most residential doors. If your door opens and closes more than 6 times per day, every 6 months is better. Regular maintenance catches small issues before they become expensive repairs.

My door makes a grinding noise — do I need a new opener? +

Not necessarily. Grinding often means worn drive gears inside the opener, which can be replaced for a fraction of the cost of a new unit. In other cases, it's the rollers or hinges. Let Edgar inspect it — he'll tell you exactly what it is.

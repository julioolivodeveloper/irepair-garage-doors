If you woke up this morning to a garage door that won't open — and you heard a loud bang last night — there's a good chance you have a broken spring. Spring failure is the single most common garage door repair call we receive in San Antonio, and it's something we fix every single day. Here's what it costs in 2026 and everything else you need to know.

Torsion Springs vs. Extension Springs: What's the Difference?

Torsion Springs

Torsion springs are mounted horizontally above the garage door opening on a steel shaft. They store energy by winding (twisting) as the door closes, then release that energy to help lift the door. Most modern San Antonio homes built in the last 20 years have torsion spring systems. They're more durable, more balanced, and safer than extension springs when they break.

Extension Springs

Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks on each side of the door. They stretch and extend as the door closes. They're more common in older homes and lighter single-car doors. When one breaks, the other side is now under uneven load — which is why we recommend replacing both sides simultaneously.

Factors That Affect Spring Replacement Cost

  • Door size and weight: A heavy double-car door (18x7 or 18x8) requires heavier-duty springs rated for higher tension, which cost more than standard springs.
  • Spring type and cycle rating: We install high-cycle springs rated for 25,000+ cycles as our standard (vs. the 10,000-cycle springs installed by builders). The materials cost a bit more but the longevity is dramatically better.
  • Same-day urgency: If you need us at 6 PM on a Friday, there's a small premium. We're upfront about it — no surprise charges.
  • Simultaneous repairs: If your cables are worn or frayed at the same time, we'll often bundle the repairs at a reduced combined rate.

Why You Should NEVER DIY Garage Door Spring Replacement

We understand the appeal of saving money — but this is one repair you should never attempt yourself. Torsion springs are under extreme mechanical tension — enough to cause severe lacerations, broken bones, or worse if released improperly. The Consumer Product Safety Commission and OSHA both document hundreds of injuries annually from improperly handled garage door springs.

The tools required (winding bars, proper tensioning knowledge, spring sizing expertise) are not something the average homeowner has. A $25 savings attempt that lands you in the emergency room is not a good trade. Call us — it's a fast repair and the price is fair.

How Long Do New Springs Last?

Standard builder-grade springs last approximately 10,000 cycles (one cycle = one open + one close). A family that uses the garage 4 times a day hits 10,000 cycles in about 7 years. iRepair installs high-cycle springs rated for 25,000 cycles as our default — that's roughly 17 years of normal use. We also offer 50,000-cycle springs for homes with very heavy use.
